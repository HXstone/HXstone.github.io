Why I keep making tiny games

2026-09-18EN

Minesweeper. A dino that never stops running. I did not write the code for either of them — and I still think of them as mine.

I don't really write code

I am a beginner. I cannot sit down and type out a game from scratch. What I can do is describe one: how it should look, what happens when you press space, how hard it should get. Then I let AI build it, play it, and ask for changes until it feels right. People call this vibe coding, and it turns out it is enough to finish small things.

Small games, small wins

A tiny game is a complete thing. It has a beginning, a set of rules and an end. That makes it the perfect size for someone like me: I can keep the whole idea in my head, see every change immediately, and actually reach the finish line.

What I have learned so far

  • Be specific. "Make it fun" gets you nowhere. "The jump should feel lighter, and the duck should be quicker" does.
  • Testing is the real work. Playing your own game badly, over and over, is how it gets better.
  • Finishing is a skill. It is easy to start ten projects and finish none. Small enough means finishable.

Done is better than perfect — and tiny is the easiest way to get to done.

What's next

More small things, probably. Maybe a puzzle, maybe a tool. Whatever it is, it will show up on the projects page.
