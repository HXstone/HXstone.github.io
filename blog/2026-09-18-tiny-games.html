Why I keep building tiny games

2026-09-18EN

Minesweeper. A dino that never stops running. None of these will change the world, and that is exactly the point.

Small games, small wins

A tiny game is a complete thing. It has a beginning, a set of rules and an end. You can hold the whole idea in your head at once, which makes it perfect for learning: every bug is yours to find, and every improvement is immediately visible on screen.

What Minesweeper taught me

Building Minesweeper was mostly an exercise in state. Which cells are open, which are flagged, where the mines hide, whether the first click should be safe. I learned that the hard part of a game is never the drawing — it is keeping your data honest.

Finishing is a skill

It is easy to start ten projects and finish none. A tiny game is small enough to actually finish, and finishing teaches you things starting never will: polish, edge cases, and the discipline to stop adding features.

Done is better than perfect — and tiny is the easiest way to get to done.

What's next

More small things, probably. Maybe a puzzle, maybe a tool. Whatever it is, it will show up on the projects page.
